Updates install themselves, and album covers show up for a lot more music, especially hip hop.

## What's new

- **Automatic updates**: when a new version comes out, the app downloads it, checks it isn't damaged, installs it and starts again, then tells you it's updated. Your settings stay as they are. To choose when to update instead, turn off **Install Updates Automatically** in the menu: an **Install Update** item then appears at the top of the menu when there's a new version. **Check for Updates…** installs a new version straight away.
- **Album covers for explicit albums**: Apple's search leaves out explicit albums that have no clean version, so songs from albums like *IGOR*, *The Money Store*, *Whole Lotta Red* or *Circles* showed the Apple Music logo instead of their cover. The app now finds them through the artist's catalog. In a test with 29 hip hop songs, every one got its cover; with Death Grips, 12 of 14 songs did, against 1 before (the other two are an album Apple Music doesn't have, and one Apple's rate limit blocked during the test, which the app retries).
- **Collaborations**: songs by "Dave & Central Cee" and the like are found under the first artist when Apple has no artist for the pair.
- After the first song by an artist, the next ones need fewer lookups, which keeps the app under Apple's limit of about 20 lookups a minute when you skip through an album.
- **More animated covers**: 95 popular hip hop albums, new and classic, were added to the animated covers, on top of the charts. Every version of the app gets those already.

This is the first version that updates itself, so install this one by hand: the next ones install themselves.

**Mac:** keep the app in **Applications**: it can't update itself while it runs from **Downloads**. After an update, macOS may ask again whether AppleMusicRPC can control Music, because the app isn't signed with an Apple developer certificate: click **OK**.

**Mac:** unzip, drag **AppleMusicRPC** into **Applications** (replacing the old one) and open it. macOS will say it can't verify the app: click **Done**, then go to **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**. When asked whether AppleMusicRPC can control Music, click **Allow**.

**Windows:** quit the old version if it's running, unzip, open the **AppleMusicRPC** folder and run **AppleMusicRPC.exe**. If SmartScreen warns you, click **More info → Run anyway**.

**Linux:** quit the old version if it's running, unzip, open the **AppleMusicRPC** folder and run **AppleMusicRPC**.
